## Summary of the contribution (for a cover letter, about 150 words)

Charles Booth's 1898 to 1899 map of London poverty is a famous record of social status at the scale of the individual street, but its legacy is usually discussed at the scale of districts. This paper reads the map's seven colours automatically along today's OpenStreetMap streets for the whole of inner London, validates the reading against a blind sample labelled by eye, and tests, following an analysis plan written before the deprivation data were joined, whether the streets Booth coloured poor are more deprived today. Across 1,221 small areas the association with the Index of Multiple Deprivation 2025 is positive but modest (rank correlation 0.20), holds within boroughs and north of the Thames but not south of it, and is highly local: sliding the historical map by one kilometre removes most of it. In an exploratory analysis, the wealthy end of Booth's scale appears more persistent than the poor end. The street-level data and code are released openly.
